📖 Background¶

You work for an international HR consultancy helping companies attract and retain top talent in the competitive tech industry. As part of your services, you provide clients with insights into industry salary trends to ensure they remain competitive in hiring and compensation practices.

Your team wants to use a data-driven approach to analyse how various factors—such as job role, experience level, remote work, and company size—impact salaries globally. By understanding these trends, you can assist in advising clients on offering competitive packages to attract the best talent.

In this project competition, which consists of three levels or parts, salary data from thousands of employees worldwide will be explored, mined, & visualized. Subsequent levels of this project will involve building predictive models to uncover key salary drivers and providing insights on how to enhance future data collection.

💾 The Data¶

The data, available in salaries.csv, comes from a survey hosted by the aforementioned HR consultancy. Each data point represents a single employee's salary record for a given year.

Column Description
work_year The year the salary was paid.
experience_level Employee experience level. EN: Entry-level / Junior, MI: Mid-level / Intermediate, SE: Senior / Expert, EX: Executive / Director
employment_type Employment type. PT: Part-time, FT: Full-time, CT: Contract, FL: Freelance
job_title The job title during the year.
salary Gross salary paid (in local currency).
salary_currency Salary currency (ISO 4217 code).
salary_in_usd Salary converted to USD using average yearly FX rate.
employee_residence Employee's primary country of residence (ISO 3166 code).
remote_ratio Percentage of remote work. 0: No remote work (<20%), 50: Hybrid (50%), 100: Fully remote (>80%)
company_location Employer's main office location (ISO 3166 code).
company_size Company size. S: Small (<50 employees), M: Medium (50–250 employees), L: Large (>250 employees)

Summary of Findings & Recommendations¶

This project (part one of three) analyzed over 57,000 survey responses of individuals within the tech industry regarding their employment details between 2020-2024. As no preprocessing was necessary, the analyses examined specific subsets of individuals & their salaries. In particular, employment information of data scientists & data engineers were compared, then ratios & salaries of people based in the US who work full-time & fully remotely were evaluated. In assessing such individuals, a deeper understanding as to some recent/current trends of employees & salaries within the tech industry was uncovered. Such information can be used to assist companies within the industry in attracting the best candidates & to help drive success.

Analysis I - Number of Annual Records¶

The HR consultancy obtained 57,194 survey responses from individuals across the globe between 2020-2024. More responses were obtained each year. In particular, 2024 accounted for almost 82% of the survey responses & 2023-2024 accounted for around 95% of them. There were only 75 responses in the first year that this survey was conducted (2020). The bias in these data points towards more recent years demonstrates the relevancy of much of this data therefore making it more reliable.

Analysis II - Salaries of Data Scientists, Data Engineers¶

Of the 57,194 survey responses, 17,676 were from data scientists & data engineers. Over the five years of records, the average salary of data scientists (~ \$159,000) was about \\$10,000 more than that of data engineers (~ \$149,000), the former of which is roughly equivalent to the overall average salary across the 57,194 responses.
When evaluated on a yearly basis, both positions saw an overall increase in their average salary between 2020 & 2024. That being said, the annual average salary of data engineers increased incrementally each year, whereas that of data scientists decreased from 2020 to 2021 & 2023 to 2024. This might be indicative that data engineer salaries are slightly less volatile & more predictable than those of data scientists. Regardless, the average salary of data scientists was greater than that of data engineers in four of the five years of records.

Based on these trends, rough estimates can be generated regarding the potential future salaries of these individuals. While typical salaries of both positions should be expected to continue to grow in the coming years, less growth in the last three years (2022-2024) might put salary estimates in 2030 roughly around \$180,000 & \\$220,000 for data engineers & data scientists respectively. In particular, the bumpier trends in reported salaries of data scientists will make forecasting their salaries more difficult.

Analysis III - Full-time Remote Workers¶

Of the 57,194 responses, 11,125 were from employees who worked fully remote, full-time, & were US-based between 2020-2024. Over this period, the average salary of such individuals was about \$155,000, slightly less than that of the overall average reported salary (~ \\$159,000).
When evaluated on a yearly basis, the recorded average salaries of these individuals were relatively consistent as there was only a ~\$1,800-overall increase from 2020 to 2024. Apart from a slight dip of about \\$12,000 from 2020 to 2021, the average annual salaries were between \$151,000-\\$156,000 for these employees across four of these five years. Moreover, this average only changed by about -\$240 in the most recent two years. Such consistency could help provide a competitive, reliable baseline for companies looking to hire such individuals.

Based on these trends, rough estimates can be generated regarding the potential future salaries of these individuals. Given the very small growth rate observed across the reported salaries of these individuals, their salaries in 2030 would be projected at around about \$166,000.

Furthermore, the proportions of US-based, full-time employees who worked fully remotely were evaluated over the five-year period to see how the rates evolved. Over the first three years, the rate of these individuals who worked entirely remotely increased--more than two people of every five worked fully remotely in 2022--but this rate declined precipitously from 2022-2024. It was hypothesized that these trends were related to the COVID-19 pandemic (which was most pressing in 2020) given that more people were forced to work from home but were eventually able to return back to "normalcy." Alternatively, such trends could point to employees &/or companies having less desire for fully-remote work. Regardless, the most recent trend points to fully-remote work becoming less popular among these individuals.

Recommendations¶

Of the particular subsets of individuals whose salaries were analyzed, client companies would be advised to offer salaries around or just above the respective averages in order to be more appealing to potential candidates & remain competitive in hiring within the tech industry. In doing so, applicants might be more likely to engage with & pursue job openings at such companies because competitive salaries can make candidates feel that their skills are valued appropriately. If companies offer salaries that are noticeably below average or if they are generally inflexible in what they are willing to offer, individuals might be less inclined to pursue such a job opening & instead look elsewhere.

Based on trends observed over the period of 2020-2024, salaries across the two subsets of individuals are generally proximal, but there are still some notable differences. For data scientists & data engineers, the former have been typically paid slightly more in recent years. Following with the recommendations listed above, companies looking to hire such individuals would be advised to offer salaries around or just above the respective averages observed in the survey responses. More specifically, data scientists would see a typical salary around or just above \$159,000, whereas data engineers would see a typical salary around or just above \\$149,000. Come 2030, salaries of these positions are projected to rise to roughly \$220,000 & \\$180,000 respectively based on recent trends.

When looking at a another group of individuals, particularly those who are based in the US, work full-time & fully remotely, a strong baseline in what salary to offer them would be around \$155,000. Employers would be encouraged to not be afraid to offer slightly more, particularly given that this number is slightly below the reported average salary across the 57,000-plus responses obtained from individuals within the tech industry. That being said, the considerably small rate of growth in these salaries from 2020-2024 can potentially give employers more predictability in the hiring market of such individuals. More specifically, salaries of US-based, fully remote, full-time employees are projected to reach around \\$166,000 in 2030 based on recent trends.

On another note, working entirely remotely seems to becoming less popular amongst people based in the US who work full time. For companies who do not include benefits to work 100% remotely, this would seem to be a positive trend. For companies whose employees generally work remotely, this could make attracting such individuals more difficult. A potential solution may be to be even more generous than previously recommended when it comes to salary offers. For example, such a company may need to consider salaries in excess of \$160,000 rather than sticking closer to \\$155,000.

For sake of transparency, the projected salary estimates in 2030 of the two subsets of individuals are only based on five years of reported data. While these estimates can be used as rough projections, companies should remain flexible & adaptable to potential market changes that could considerably impact employees' salaries & desires from a job. As a recent example, the COVID-19 pandemic (2020) drastically impacted ways in which people work as well as what kind of benefits they might seek in a new job particularly when it comes to working remotely. As such, hiring practices have adapted in many cases.
Ultimately, salaries & such job benefits may depend on the level of the position being offered & how desperately it needs to be filled; however, by providing potential applicants with stronger & more appealing salaries & benefits, it will help such companies to remain competitive within their industry & attract the best talent.

In the first plot, there is a somewhat positive, linear trend in the average annual salary of these 11,125 data points, specifically from 2021-2023; however, the annual average declines from 2020 to 2021 & 2023 to 2024 (very slightly). This trend is similar to that of the average salaries for data scientists as seen in Analysis II. With that being said, the average annual salaries of these records are all between \$141,000-\\$156,000. Furthermore, excluding 2021, these salaries are between \$151,000-\\$156,000. The figure above illustrates the annual proportion of responses which were from employees who worked from the US, full-time, & fully remotely between 2020-2024. Of the 57,125 responses during this period, 11,125 pertain to such individuals. Annual proportions were utilized because the number of responses over each year dramatically increased (20 in 2020 to 8,040 in 2024).

Over the first three years, working fully remotely became more popular for full-time employees in the US. Nearly half worked fully remotely in 2022; however, this rate severely declined over the following two years. As such, it might suggest that working only remotely became less desirable for employees &/or companies. Perhaps this trend relates to the general conclusions of the immediate aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic which became a pressing matter in 2020. As it ravaged communities & the public, employees were forced to work from home more, but when it was deemed "safer" to return to normalcy, more people preferred to work at the office or partially remotely rather than completely remotely.
Alternatively, this trend might point to companies promoting & hiring less jobs that include the benefit of working fully remotely. Regardless, this trend points to fully-remote work becoming less popular among US-based, full-time employees. It would be interesting to have data on this before 2020 to see what these levels were like before the pandemic began severely impacting society. It could also give an idea as to how these rates might evolve in the near future; will it continue to decrease or will it level out.
